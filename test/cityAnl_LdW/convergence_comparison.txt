Comparison: cityAnl_LdW vs windAroundBuildings
==============================================

Conclusion
----------
The solver logs do not support the claim that windAroundBuildings has better
overall SIMPLE residual convergence. At matched iterations 118 and 200,
cityAnl_LdW has lower pressure residuals and much smaller continuity errors.
Some velocity components are lower in the wind case, so no single field tells
the whole story.

The wind case can still look cleaner or more stable in results. The strongest
evidence for a possible cause is mesh quality: the current city mesh fails two
checkMesh quality checks, while its older snappyHexMesh log aborted during
refinement. The wind snappyHexMesh log reports successful meshing and no
failures for the quality limits it checks. The outlet boundary conditions also
differ and may matter if there is reverse flow. These are plausible causes of
differences in the solution, not proof that they caused the observed residuals.

Why apparent convergence/performance can differ
------------------------------------------------
* The runs have different lengths because controlDict sets endTime to 200 for
  city and 400 for wind. Both logs end normally at that limit; neither stopped
  because it met the configured 1e-4 residualControl targets. The logs are
  therefore not evidence that the wind case converged sooner.
* Wind is substantially cheaper per iteration: about 0.725 s/iteration by
  clock time, versus 2.985 s/iteration for city. The current city mesh has
  600,363 cells; the wind snappyHexMesh log reports 185,237 cells. This
  roughly 3.2x cell-count difference accounts for much of the runtime
  difference, though it does not by itself prove the full cause.
* A checkMesh run on the city mesh currently on disk (Oct 31) found 600,363
  cells, maximum skewness 7.22086 with 195 highly skewed faces, and 33,444
  concave cells. It reported "Failed 2 mesh checks." Topology and cell volumes
  passed. The wind snappyHexMesh log (Oct 27) reports successful meshing and
  zero faces beyond its listed skewness, non-orthogonality, and other limits;
  that log is not a fresh checkMesh run on the current wind mesh.
* The city snappyHexMesh log is older (Oct 27) and ends with a fatal error
  during shell refinement. The city polyMesh files currently on disk were
  modified Oct 30, before the Oct 30 simpleFoam run. Treat that old failed
  meshing attempt and the newer solver run as separate evidence.
* Both current cases use simpleFoam, RAS kEpsilon, nu = 1.5e-5, the same
  fvSchemes pattern, and 1e-4 residualControl targets. Current pressure
  relaxation differs: 0.6 for city and 0.3 for wind; U and turbulence
  equation relaxation are 0.7 in both.
* Inlet scales differ substantially: city U = 1 m/s, k = 0.00375,
  epsilon = 0.000004; wind U = 10 m/s, k = 1.5, epsilon = 0.03. These
  normalized residual histories are not a controlled comparison of physical
  solution error across the two different flow setups.
* Outlet U, k, and epsilon are zeroGradient in city. Wind uses
  pressureInletOutletVelocity for U and inletOutlet for k and epsilon, which
  accommodates reverse flow. This is worth investigating only if city has
  reverse outlet flux; the logs here do not establish that it does.

Recommended next checks
-----------------------
1. Fix/regenerate the city mesh and require current checkMesh results to pass
   the intended quality criteria. Confirm a fresh snappyHexMesh run completes.
2. Then rerun both cases with saved dictionaries, comparable iteration limits,
   and the same residual/continuity reporting. Compare all fields, not just p.
3. Check outlet flux for reverse flow before changing city outlet conditions.

Do not infer that wind has better overall solver convergence from smoother
plots or shorter runtime. The current logs show cheaper iterations for wind,
but lower pressure residuals and continuity errors for city at matched
iterations; the cases also differ in mesh quality, boundary conditions, and
inlet/turbulence scales.
